CHAPTER 1 THE BIRTH OF A DIGITAL UNIVERSE


In 1989 Tim BernersLee sent a groundbreaking proposal to his supervisors at CERN
The subject line read Information Management A Proposal Nobody knew it then but
that single document  shared via email at infocernch  would ignite an explosion
of data unlike anything humanity had ever seen Visit httpshomecernchsciencecomputingbirthweb
to read the original proposal Within just 10 years the World Wide Web had connected
over 300000000 people By 2005 internet users were generating MORE than 2 EXABYTES
of data every single day

CHAPTER 2 THE THREE Vs  AND THEN SOME


Doug Laney an analyst at META Group now Gartner first described Big Data using
3 defining characteristics in 2001

  1 VOLUME    The sheer SIZE of data think petabytes zettabytes
  2 VELOCITY  The SPEED at which data is generated eg 500000 tweetsminute
  3 VARIETY   The TYPES of data structured semistructured and unstructured

Later researchers at IBM added a 4th V VERACITY  the trustworthiness of data
Dr Kirk Borne kbornegmuedu expanded this to 10 Vs by 2017 For the full list
see httpswwwdatasciencecentralcomthevofbigdata

CHAPTER 3 HADOOP  THE ELEPHANT THAT CHANGED EVERYTHING


In 2006 Doug Cutting and Mike Cafarella released Apache Hadoop  named after Dougs
sons toy elephant  under the Apache Software Foundation Hadoop allowed companies
to store and process MASSIVE datasets across clusters of 1000s of commodity servers

   Yahoo ran Hadoop on 42000 nodes by 2011
   Facebook stored 100 PETABYTES of data using Hadoop by 2012
   Contribute or learn more at httpshadoopapacheorg or email devhadoopapacheorg

The release of Hadoop 10 on November 16 2011 was a watershed moment Suddenly
organizations with budgets under 500000 could process TERABYTES of data overnight
THAT was revolutionary

CHAPTER 4 GOOGLE AND THE MAPREDUCE REVOLUTION


Before Hadoop Google engineers Jeffrey Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat published a landmark
paper titled MapReduce Simplified Data Processing on Large Clusters in 2004
Download it here httpsresearchgooglecomarchivemapreduceosdi04pdf

The core idea DIVIDE a problem into small chunks MAP process them in PARALLEL
then COMBINE the results REDUCE Using this approach Google indexed 8058044651
web pages  over 8 BILLION  in under 10 hours Questions Reach their research team
at researchpapersgooglecom

CHAPTER 7 THE DARK SIDE  PRIVACY  ETHICS


NOT everything about Big Data is triumphant In March 2018 it emerged that Cambridge
Analytica harvested the personal data of 87000000 Facebook users WITHOUT their
consent The scandal triggered GDPR General Data Protection Regulation which took
effect on May 25 2018 carrying fines of up to 20000000 or 4 of global turnover

The EUs data protection authority can be reached at httpsedpbeuropaeu or
contactedpbeuropaeu Meanwhile the California Consumer Privacy Act CCPA
went into force on January 1 2020  giving 39000000 Californians new rights
over their personal data PRIVACY IS NOT OPTIONAL IT IS A FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT
